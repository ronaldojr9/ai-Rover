Optimize My Production: How ERP Solved Our Shop Floor Challenges

A real-world look at how a small job shop tackled production delays, scheduling conflicts, and inventory blind spots using Rover ERP's production management tools.

The Challenge: A Job Shop Running on Gut Feel

For a 35-person job shop producing custom metal fabrications, the warning signs had been building for years. Lead times were creeping up. Customers were calling to check on order status because nobody inside the shop could answer the question quickly. Parts shortages were holding up jobs at the worst possible moments — often discovered only when a machinist pulled a work order and went looking for material that was not there.

Scheduling was done on a whiteboard and in spreadsheets, updated manually when someone remembered. Work center capacity was managed by instinct. When a new rush job came in, the production manager had no reliable way to know whether accepting it would push existing commitments past their due dates. Paper-based work orders meant that job status existed only in people's heads — and that information disappeared every time someone was out sick or left the company.

The shop was profitable, but growth was stalled. Taking on more work meant more chaos, not more revenue. Something had to change.

The Solution: Rover ERP Manufacturing and Inventory Modules

After evaluating several ERP platforms, the shop selected Rover ERP and went live with the Manufacturing and Inventory modules in a phased rollout over 90 days. The implementation team started by digitizing all BOMs and routings — a labor-intensive step that paid dividends almost immediately by surfacing errors and outdated specifications that had been silently causing problems for years.

Key capabilities deployed in the initial rollout included:

  • Work Order Management with visual status tracking. Every job in the shop now has a digital work order with real-time status visible to anyone with system access.
  • Gantt-based scheduling. The production planner can drag and drop jobs across work centers, see conflicts instantly, and use auto-push to resolve downstream scheduling issues without manual recalculation.
  • Real-time inventory lookup on the shop floor. Machinists can check material availability before pulling a work order, eliminating the wasted trips to the stockroom for parts that are not there.
  • BOM and routing management. Engineering changes are now controlled and versioned, so production always works from current specifications.

Feature Spotlight: The Production Board

Of all the capabilities rolled out, the Production Board view generated the most immediate enthusiasm from supervisors. The Production Board gives shop floor management a real-time visual dashboard of all active work orders organized by work center — what is scheduled, what is in progress, and what is completed.

Color-coded sub-status indicators make it possible to identify at-risk jobs at a glance, without drilling into individual records. When a work center is overloaded or a job is falling behind, the board surfaces that information in seconds rather than requiring a physical walk-through of the floor.

"Before Rover, I had to walk the floor to know where jobs were. Now I can see everything from my desk and catch problems before they become delays."

— Production Supervisor, custom metal fabrication shop

Work Order Split: Flexibility for Complex Jobs

One feature that proved unexpectedly valuable was the Work Order Split capability. For large orders that were tying up work centers for extended periods, the ability to split a work order into smaller, more manageable units gave the scheduler new flexibility. Partial quantities could be moved through the shop on different schedules, allowing partial shipments to customers and better load balancing across work centers.

Results After Six Months

Six months after go-live, the results were measurable and material:

20%
Reduction in average production lead times
15%
Improvement in on-time delivery rate
0
Emergency parts runs in the last 60 days

Beyond the numbers, the culture of the shop floor changed. Production decisions moved from reactive to proactive. Supervisors spent less time firefighting and more time planning. Customer calls about order status dropped because the sales team could now answer those questions directly from the system — without interrupting production.

The production manager summed it up simply: "We are not working harder. We are working with better information."
