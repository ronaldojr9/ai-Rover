Rover ERP at SEMA 2025: Reuniting, Innovating & Capturing the Spirit of the Aftermarket

The Rover ERP team had an incredible time at SEMA 2025 in Las Vegas — catching up with builders, manufacturers, and shop owners we support every day.

There is no trade show in the automotive world quite like SEMA. The 2025 edition of the Specialty Equipment Market Association Show once again transformed the Las Vegas Convention Center into a four-day showcase of everything that makes the automotive aftermarket one of the most dynamic and passionate industries on the planet. From November 4 through 7, more than 160,000 attendees representing over 100 countries descended on Las Vegas, joining upwards of 2,400 exhibiting brands across millions of square feet of convention space. For the Rover ERP team, it was a homecoming.

The Rover Team on the Floor

Bill Baker, Jim Hammons, and the broader Rover crew made the trip to Las Vegas to do what they do best: connect. SEMA is not just a trade show for our team — it is an annual opportunity to sit down with the manufacturers, distributors, and shop owners who rely on Rover ERP every day to keep their operations running. We walked the halls, joined conversations in booths, and found the kind of candid feedback that no survey can replicate. There is something irreplaceable about standing next to a customer in front of a custom build and talking shop — literally.

The Themes That Defined SEMA 2025

Every SEMA tells a story about where the industry is headed, and 2025 was no exception. A few themes stood out across the show floor:

  • Classic design meets cutting-edge technology. The tension — and harmony — between heritage aesthetics and modern engineering was everywhere. Resto-mods with billet aluminum components, digital dashboards tucked inside hand-stitched leather interiors, and OBD-integrated data systems hiding behind patina paint jobs all made appearances.
  • EV conversions going mainstream. Electric drivetrain swaps, once a niche curiosity, had a commanding presence at SEMA 2025. Builders are increasingly treating the EV conversion as just another form of customization, with dedicated motor kits, battery management systems, and charging solutions designed specifically for classic vehicles.
  • Advanced diagnostics and shop intelligence. The professional installer and shop owner audience at SEMA has grown increasingly sophisticated. Diagnostic tools, shop management integrations, and fleet telematics platforms drew serious crowds — a sign that the back office is catching up to the build quality on the floor.
  • Customization culture is thriving. Despite supply chain pressures in recent years, the custom aftermarket is not slowing down. Fabricators, specialty parts manufacturers, and boutique accessory brands brought products that demonstrated genuine craftsmanship and ingenuity.

"Walking the floor at SEMA, you feel the energy immediately — every booth is telling a story, every builder is solving a problem in a new way. That relentless drive to improve and innovate is exactly what our customers in the aftermarket bring to their businesses every day, and it's what we try to reflect in how we build Rover ERP."

— Bill Baker, Rover ERP

How Rover ERP Serves the Automotive Aftermarket

The automotive aftermarket is a uniquely demanding environment for an ERP platform. Parts catalogs run into the tens of thousands of SKUs. Lead times from overseas suppliers fluctuate unpredictably. Order patterns are seasonal, build-specific, and highly variable. Kitting, sub-assembly, and custom fabrication workflows sit alongside standard distribution operations. It is a world where flexibility is not a nice-to-have — it is a requirement.

Rover ERP is built to handle that complexity without punishing the operators who have to live inside the system every day. Key capabilities our aftermarket customers depend on include:

  • Flexible bill of materials and work orders that accommodate both standard production runs and one-off custom builds without forcing operators to create entirely separate workflows.
  • Multi-warehouse inventory management with real-time visibility across locations, enabling fast fulfillment even when parts are distributed across multiple facilities.
  • Integration with shop management systems to keep the front-of-house customer experience connected to back-office procurement, inventory, and accounting.
  • Serial and lot tracking for high-value or safety-critical components, supporting the traceability requirements that come with performance and safety parts.

Looking Ahead to 2026

SEMA 2025 reinforced what the Rover team already knows: the automotive aftermarket is not standing still, and neither are we. The conversations we had on the show floor — about growth plans, operational bottlenecks, and the technology gaps that are holding shops and manufacturers back — will directly inform how we continue to develop the platform in 2026. We came back from Las Vegas energized, with a full notebook of customer insights and a renewed commitment to building software that is worthy of the craftspeople and entrepreneurs who use it.

If you stopped by to talk with us at SEMA 2025, thank you. If we missed you, we look forward to connecting in 2026 — on the show floor or anytime in between.
