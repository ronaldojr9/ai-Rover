Bill & Jim's Excellent Adventure to SEMA 2024

Bill Baker and Jim Hammons share their colorful insights from SEMA 2024 — an automotive wonderland that showcased the industry's most exciting innovations and brought together leaders across the aftermarket.

Every year, Las Vegas transforms into the automotive capital of the world for the SEMA Show — and 2024 was no exception. Bill Baker and Jim Hammons from the Rover ERP team made the trip out to the Las Vegas Convention Center in late October and early November to walk the floor, connect with customers, and take the pulse of where the automotive aftermarket is heading. Here's what they found.

An Automotive Wonderland — Every Inch Packed

SEMA 2024 was massive. Every convention hall at the Las Vegas Convention Center was completely full, with overflow exhibits spilling into nearby hotels and venues across the Strip. The scale of the show is always impressive, but this year felt different.

"Every single convention hall was completely full, plus they had things at some of the hotels and other places. Even our secret parking lot was full — that's never happened before."

— Jim Hammons, Rover ERP

When your longtime "secret" parking spot is gone, you know the show has grown. The energy matched the attendance: exhibitors were energized, buyers were engaged, and the floor buzzed with the kind of excitement that only comes from an industry that genuinely loves its products.

Key Trends From the Floor

Classic Meets Modern: The EV Fusion Wave

One of the most striking trends at SEMA 2024 was the fusion of classic automotive design with cutting-edge technology. Vintage muscle cars with modern EV drivetrains. Classic styling paired with fully digital dashboards and advanced driver assistance features. The aesthetic of the golden age of American cars — but with the performance and efficiency of the present.

EV conversion kits are becoming genuinely mainstream. What was once the domain of well-funded custom shops is now accessible as bolt-on solutions for converting beloved classics to electric power — at price points that don't require a second mortgage. Multiple vendors on the floor were offering complete conversion packages with straightforward installation guides.

AI-Powered Diagnostics

Advanced diagnostics tools were prominently featured throughout the show, with a clear shift toward AI-powered systems that can predict failures before they happen. Rather than simply reading fault codes, these tools analyze patterns over time — identifying components that are trending toward failure before the customer ever notices a symptom.

For aftermarket parts suppliers and distributors, this shift has real implications: demand will increasingly be driven by predictive replacement cycles rather than reactive repairs.

Customization Culture Is Stronger Than Ever

If there was any worry that the push toward EVs and technology would dull the customization culture at the heart of SEMA, those worries were put to rest. Unique builds, one-off creations, and personalization at every price point were everywhere. From wild show trucks to subtle but precise suspension upgrades, the desire to make a vehicle uniquely yours remains the beating heart of this industry.

What It Means for Rover ERP Customers in the Aftermarket

Walking the floor with an ERP lens, several themes emerged that directly affect how aftermarket manufacturers and distributors need to run their operations:

Three Aftermarket Imperatives for 2025

Rapid product change cycles, complex part relationships, and tighter shop-to-back-office integration are the operational challenges that will define competitive advantage in the automotive aftermarket over the next few years.

  • Rapid product change cycles demand flexible inventory management. New products, new fitments, and new technology mean your parts catalog is never static. Your ERP needs to handle continuous catalog updates without disrupting ongoing operations.
  • Complex part relationships need robust ERP support. Kits, bundles, and fitment data are the lifeblood of the aftermarket. Managing the relationships between parts — what fits what, what goes with what — is a core ERP function that separates purpose-built solutions from generic platforms.
  • Integration between shop management and back-office ERP is no longer optional. As diagnostics and shop management systems become more sophisticated, the demand for seamless data flow to back-office inventory, purchasing, and accounting is growing rapidly.

The Rover ERP Automotive Advantage

Rover ERP has served the automotive aftermarket for years, and the platform reflects that experience. Flexible workflow customization lets you adapt processes to your specific business model — whether you're a manufacturer, a distributor, or a combination of both. Kit and bundle inventory support handles complex part relationships without workarounds. And Rover's integration capabilities mean you can connect your ERP to the shop management systems, e-commerce platforms, and diagnostic tools your customers are increasingly relying on.

We came back from SEMA energized and full of ideas. The industry is healthy, innovative, and hungry for better tools. We're looking forward to SEMA 2025 — and to continuing to serve the aftermarket community between now and then.
