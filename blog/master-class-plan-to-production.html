Rover ERP Master Class – From Plan to Production

In this month's Rover ERP Master Class, we focused on the production planning process — helping manufacturing teams streamline the journey from demand signal to finished goods.

For manufacturers, the gap between a plan and actual production is where costs grow, schedules slip, and customer commitments get missed. In October's Master Class, we walked through Rover ERP's production planning tools end-to-end — from demand signals and material requirements planning through work order execution and completion.

What We Covered

This session covered the complete production planning cycle in Rover ERP: Demand Planning (sales forecasts, sales orders, and MPS), Material Requirements Planning (MRP runs, planned order generation, and exception handling), and Work Order Execution (release, dispatch, shop floor reporting, and completion).

Demand Planning: Where the Plan Starts

Every production plan starts with demand. In Rover ERP, demand comes from multiple sources that are consolidated into the Master Production Schedule (MPS):

  • Firm demand: Open sales orders with confirmed delivery dates
  • Forecast demand: Manually entered or imported sales forecasts
  • Interplant demand: Transfer orders and branch replenishment requests
  • Safety stock replenishment: Triggered when on-hand quantities fall below defined minimums

The MPS view in Rover Web lets planners see all demand sources consolidated by item and time bucket — giving a clear picture of what needs to be produced and when, before MRP is run.

Material Requirements Planning (MRP)

MRP is where Rover ERP translates demand into action. In the session, we walked through the complete MRP run process:

Running MRP

  1. Select the planning horizon (days out to plan)
  2. Select which items and locations to include
  3. Run the calculation — Rover ERP explodes BOMs, checks on-hand and on-order quantities, and generates planned orders to cover net requirements
  4. Review the output in the MRP exception report before releasing any planned orders

MRP Exception Handling

The exception report is where most of the planning work happens. Rover ERP surfaces three types of exceptions:

  • Past due: Planned orders that are already late given current lead times — need immediate action
  • Reschedule in: Existing orders scheduled too far out — can be moved earlier to better align with demand
  • Reschedule out or cancel: Existing orders that are ahead of need — can be delayed or cancelled to free up capacity or reduce WIP

In the demo, we showed how planners can review and action MRP exceptions directly in Rover Web — approving, rescheduling, or deleting planned orders without leaving the planning screen.

Work Order Execution

Once planned orders are approved and converted to work orders, the focus shifts to the shop floor. We covered the complete work order lifecycle:

Work Order Release

  • Confirm material availability before releasing — the system flags any components below required quantity
  • Print shop packets including routing, BOM, and any attached drawings or work instructions
  • Assign to work centers based on the routing — with current queue visibility to avoid overloading

Shop Floor Reporting

  • Labor entry: Record actual hours by operation, employee, and work center
  • Material issues: Record actual material usage against work order requirements
  • Operation completion: Mark individual routing steps complete as they're finished
  • Scrap and rework: Record scrap quantities and reasons — tracked separately from good production

Work Order Completion & Variance

At completion, Rover ERP automatically calculates production variances:

  • Material variance: Actual material usage vs. standard BOM quantity
  • Labor variance: Actual labor hours and cost vs. standard routing
  • Quantity variance: Completed quantity vs. originally planned quantity

Variances are immediately visible in the work order completion screen and flow through to costing automatically.
