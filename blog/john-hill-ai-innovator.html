From High Diver to AI Innovator: How John Hill is Transforming PICK/MultiValue Development with AI

John Hill's journey from competitive diving to three decades of PICK/MultiValue development is as unconventional as his approach to AI — and both have made him one of the most innovative voices in the MultiValue community.

Most people in the PICK/MultiValue community know John Hill for his decades of technical expertise. What fewer know is that before he became a software developer, John was a competitive high diver — a discipline that rewarded precision, fearlessness, and the willingness to commit fully to a technique you'd practiced but never quite performed in exactly that moment.

Those qualities, it turns out, translate remarkably well to being an early adopter of AI in one of software development's most overlooked ecosystems.

Three Decades in PICK/MultiValue

John has been writing PICK/MultiValue code for over 30 years. He's built manufacturing systems, distribution platforms, and custom business applications for clients across industries — accumulating deep knowledge of the MultiValue data model, the BASIC programming environment, and the peculiarities of a database paradigm that most modern developers have never encountered.

That depth of knowledge put him in an interesting position when large language models started becoming genuinely useful for software development. Most AI tools are trained overwhelmingly on mainstream languages — Python, JavaScript, Java — with minimal exposure to MultiValue BASIC, UniBasic, or the idiosyncratic query language structures of PICK databases.

For most developers, this would be a limitation. For John, it was a starting point.

Teaching AI to Speak MultiValue

"The AI doesn't know PICK natively," John explains. "But it's very good at patterns. And once you learn how to give it the right context — show it what MultiValue data structures look like, explain the correlative and conversion codes, demonstrate how BASIC handles multi-values — it can generate surprisingly useful code."

His approach involves what he calls "context loading" — providing the AI with detailed descriptions of the data structures, the platform conventions, and the specific task before asking it to generate any code. The results, he says, are dramatically better than asking it cold.

He's been documenting his techniques and sharing them with the MultiValue community — through presentations, blog posts, and direct collaboration with other developers who are trying to figure out how to use these tools in a domain the tools weren't specifically designed for.

What He's Building

Beyond using AI as a coding assistant, John is exploring more ambitious applications: using AI to help maintain and document legacy PICK systems that have accumulated decades of undocumented customizations. Many MultiValue shops have critical business logic locked in code that nobody fully understands anymore — the original developers have left, the documentation was never written, and the system just runs.

"There's an enormous amount of institutional knowledge trapped in old PICK code," he says. "AI can help read that code, explain what it does, and document it in a way that's actually useful. That's incredibly valuable for organizations trying to modernize."

He's also working on AI-assisted test generation for MultiValue applications — a domain where automated testing has historically been difficult because of the lack of tooling designed for the platform.

What the Broader MultiValue Community Can Learn

John's work points to a broader opportunity for the PICK/MultiValue community: AI tools don't need to be trained on your specific platform to be useful. They need to be guided intelligently — given enough context to apply their general reasoning capabilities to your specific domain.

For manufacturers and distributors running PICK-based ERP systems like Rover ERP, this has direct implications:

  • AI can help write and review custom Rover ERP code — with the right context
  • AI can help document existing customizations that were built before documentation became standard practice
  • AI can help generate test cases for critical business logic
  • AI can help bridge the knowledge gap when experienced developers retire or move on

The Broader Point

The PICK/MultiValue community has always been characterized by deep expertise and creative problem-solving within constraints. That turns out to be exactly the right mindset for getting value from AI tools that weren't specifically designed for your platform. The developers who understand their domain deeply — and who approach AI as a tool to augment that expertise — are already ahead.
