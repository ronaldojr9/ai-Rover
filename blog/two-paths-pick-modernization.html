Two Paths to PICK Modernization: Lessons from Eagle Group & Encompass

Two companies — Eagle Group and Encompass — show us how different strategies can lead to the same goal: preserving the value of PICK while preparing for the future.

PICK/MultiValue databases have powered manufacturing and distribution operations for decades. They're fast, flexible, and deeply integrated into the business logic of the companies that run them. But they also present a modernization challenge: how do you move forward without losing decades of business logic, customizations, and institutional knowledge locked in your existing system?

Two Rover ERP customers — Eagle Group and Encompass — took meaningfully different approaches to this challenge. Both succeeded. Their stories offer useful lessons for any PICK/MultiValue shop thinking about modernization.

Eagle Group: The Gradual Migration

Eagle Group is a mid-size industrial manufacturer that had been running a legacy PICK-based ERP for over 20 years. The system worked — but it was showing its age. The interface was terminal-based, reporting required specialized knowledge that only a few people in the organization had, and integration with modern tools like EDI, ecommerce, and customer portals was manual and error-prone.

Eagle Group's leadership knew they needed to modernize, but they were unwilling to accept the risk of a "big bang" replacement. Too much critical business logic was embedded in the existing system. Too many of their processes depended on customizations that had been built over years.

Their Approach

Eagle Group chose a gradual migration path: keep the existing PICK system as the "system of record" while incrementally adding Rover ERP modules in front of it. They started with the customer-facing layer — web orders, customer portal, and EDI — while leaving their core manufacturing and inventory logic on the existing system.

Over 18 months, they migrated modules one at a time:

  1. Customer Portal and web ordering (Month 3)
  2. Sales order processing and customer master (Month 7)
  3. Inventory and warehouse management (Month 12)
  4. Purchasing and AP (Month 16)
  5. Manufacturing and production (Month 22)

Key Lessons

  • Parallel running is expensive but de-risks each phase significantly
  • Starting with customer-facing modules creates visible wins early and builds organizational confidence
  • Data migration is always harder than expected — plan for it specifically in each phase
  • Invest heavily in staff training at each phase; each module transition requires its own change management

Encompass: The Clean Cutover

Encompass is a specialty distributor that had also been on a PICK-based system for years. Unlike Eagle Group, Encompass's system was less customized — their business processes were closer to standard distribution workflows. When they evaluated the effort of a gradual migration vs. a clean cutover, they concluded that the faster path made more sense for their situation.

Their Approach

Encompass spent six months in pre-implementation: thoroughly documenting their existing processes, cleaning their data, and training a core team on Rover ERP before the go-live. Then they cut over on a Friday — running the legacy system through end of business, importing their data over the weekend, and starting Monday on Rover ERP exclusively.

Key Lessons

  • Data quality work before cutover is the highest-value investment you can make
  • A clean cutover requires a team that is genuinely ready — not just trained, but confident
  • Having a 90-day hypercare plan with intensive vendor support post-cutover is essential
  • The speed of the cutover approach means faster realization of the new system's benefits — Encompass was seeing measurable productivity improvements within 60 days

Which Path Is Right for You?

The right modernization path depends on your specific situation. Some factors that push toward gradual migration:

  • Heavily customized existing system with complex business logic
  • Large organization where change management is difficult
  • High risk tolerance for parallel cost but low tolerance for operational disruption

Factors that push toward clean cutover:

  • Relatively standard business processes with limited customization
  • Strong internal project team with capacity to support an intensive pre-implementation
  • Desire to move faster and avoid the complexity of maintaining two systems simultaneously

What Both Paths Have in Common

Despite their different approaches, Eagle Group and Encompass both invested heavily in the same things: data quality, staff training, executive sponsorship, and a clear plan for the first 90 days post-go-live. These aren't optional — they're the factors that determine success regardless of which migration path you choose.
