The BUILD Framework™ for Manufacturing AI

In this first session of our Chaos to Clarity Webinar Series, Corey Luckow and Tyler Hansen show you how to build your very first AI assistant—live, in just 45 minutes.

The question we hear from manufacturers constantly isn't "should we use AI?" It's "where do we start?" In the first session of our Chaos to Clarity Webinar Series, Corey Luckow (President, Zumasys) and Tyler Hansen (Implementation Consultant) answered that question directly — with a live demonstration that took a manufacturing AI assistant from zero to working in 45 minutes.

The methodology they used is what we call the BUILD Framework™.

About the Chaos to Clarity Series

The Chaos to Clarity Webinar Series is a multi-part program designed to help manufacturing and distribution businesses move from data chaos — scattered reports, slow decisions, disconnected systems — to operational clarity through practical AI implementations built on their existing ERP data.

The BUILD Framework™

BUILD is an acronym that describes the five-phase approach to manufacturing AI that actually gets deployed and used — not just demoed and forgotten:

B – Business Problem First

Every successful AI implementation starts with a specific, defined business problem — not with "we should use AI." What decision is being made too slowly? What question requires too much manual data gathering to answer? What process creates unnecessary delays or errors?

For manufacturers, these tend to fall into a few categories: production status visibility, inventory exception management, customer inquiry response, and supply chain risk identification. The BUILD Framework™ starts by picking one — the one that causes the most pain — and solving that first.

U – Understand Your Data

AI is only as good as the data it's built on. Before building anything, you need to understand what data you actually have, where it lives, how current it is, and whether it's trustworthy enough to act on.

For Rover ERP customers, this step is often shorter than expected — because the data is already in a structured, integrated system. But it still requires asking the right questions: Is the data actually being maintained? Are records up to date? Are statuses accurate?

I – Identify the Right Use Case

Not every business problem is a good AI use case. The best early AI use cases for manufacturers have three characteristics:

  • High frequency: The question or task comes up multiple times per day
  • Definable answer: There is a correct answer that can be derived from your ERP data — not a judgment call
  • Time-sensitive: Getting the answer faster has clear operational value

"What's the status of order X?" hits all three. "Should we expand into a new market?" hits none of them.

L – Launch with a Narrow Scope

The biggest mistake in manufacturing AI projects is trying to do too much at once. The BUILD Framework™ insists on starting narrow: one use case, one user group, one data source. Get that working well, demonstrate value, then expand.

In the live demo, Corey and Tyler built an AI assistant that could answer one type of question — order status — for one user group (internal customer service). That's it. And it was production-ready in 45 minutes.

D – Deploy, Measure, Iterate

AI projects don't end at deployment. The BUILD Framework™ includes a measurement phase: how many questions is the assistant answering per day? How often does it need to escalate to a human? What questions is it being asked that it can't yet answer?

That data drives the next iteration — expanding to the next use case, adding data sources, or improving accuracy on high-frequency queries.

The Live Demo

The session's centerpiece was a live build of a Rover AI assistant — starting from scratch and ending with a working assistant that could answer real questions against a live Rover ERP environment.

The steps demonstrated:

  1. Define the assistant's scope (order status queries for customer service)
  2. Connect the assistant to Rover ERP through Rover Data Services
  3. Configure the data entities the assistant can access (sales orders, shipments, customers)
  4. Set security boundaries (what data can be accessed, by whom)
  5. Test the assistant with real queries and review the responses
  6. Publish the assistant for the customer service team

Total time: 43 minutes from blank configuration to a working, tested, deployed assistant.
